# Chapter 4 — Deciding without you: three levels

## Lesson

You will not sit next to Claude. You will give it a slice in the morning and read the
result in the evening. Between the two, questions come up. Without a rule, every question
is a stop, and a stop waits for you for hours. With a rule, most questions are answered
on the spot and written down, and only the ones that matter reach you.

Three levels.

**Level 0, pre-approved.** The answer is already in the project's constitution, under
"Pre-approved decisions". Claude applies it and quotes the line. Example: "Letters are
always addressed to a named person; if no name is known, find one before drafting."

**Level 1, critical. Claude stops and asks.** Anything with an external consequence:
sending a message, publishing, paying, deleting outside the folder, creating or changing
a password or key. Anything irreversible on real data. Any choice that binds more than
one phase: a tool, a structure, a supplier. Anything that changes what you already
approved. Claude writes the question in one paragraph and waits.

**Level 2, reversible. Claude decides, records, continues.** A name, an order, a default,
the layout of a document, a choice between two equivalent ways. The decision goes in the
session log under "Decisions taken without the user", with a one-line way to undo it.
You read them in the evening and veto what you dislike. A veto costs one correction; a
stop would have cost half a day.

The test when unsure: can it be undone in less than one session? Then it is level 2.
If doubt remains after the test, it is level 1.

The pre-approved list is the most valuable thing you will write. Every session with a
question you had to answer should end with that answer added to the list.

## Exercise

Classify each situation as 0, 1 or 2, one word each, then I will correct:

a. Claude needs a name for the folder that holds your interview notes.
b. Claude has drafted an e-mail to an employer and could send it.
c. Your constitution says "every CV is exported to PDF before being sent"; Claude has a
   CV ready and wonders which format to use.
d. Claude thinks the whole project should be reorganised by month instead of by employer.
e. Claude is unsure whether a table should list the date before or after the company name.

## What a good answer contains

a. 2. b. 1. c. 0. d. 1 (binds more than one phase). e. 2.
Four out of five is fine; explain the missed one in two sentences.
