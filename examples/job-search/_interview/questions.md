# Ten questions of the trade

Each answer is two lines the owner validated on 2026-10-03. Say them, do not read them.

1. **A customer is angry about a cancellation that is not your fault. What do you do?**
   I let them finish, repeat the facts as they stated them, then say what I can do today
   and when they will hear from me. I write that promise down and keep it.
2. **How do you handle a question you cannot answer?**
   I say I do not know, give a time by which I will, and come back before that time even
   if the answer is still pending.
3. **Phone or e-mail: which do you prefer, and why?**
   E-mail for anything that must be exact (dates, amounts, references); the phone when the
   customer needs to be heard first. Most cases need both, in that order.
4. **A colleague gave a customer wrong information. The customer calls you.**
   I fix the customer's problem first, without blaming the colleague in front of them,
   then tell the colleague what happened so it does not repeat.
5. **How do you keep track of open cases?**
   One list, one row per case, with the next action and its date. If it is not on the
   list, it does not exist.
6. **Tell me about a procedure you improved.**
   The cancellation checklist after a supplier failure: refunds went from three weeks to
   eight working days because every step had an owner and a date.
7. **What does a good answer to a customer look like in writing?**
   The decision in the first sentence, the reason in the second, the next step and its
   date in the third. Nothing the customer has to decode.
8. **How do you switch between French and English during a day?**
   I answer in the customer's language and keep internal notes in one language only, so
   the next colleague reads the same file.
9. **What would you do in your first month here?**
   Read the last hundred tickets before answering one, to learn what your customers ask
   and how the good answers were written.
10. **Why support, and not sales?**
    Because the work is to make a promise and keep it, and I am better at keeping than at
    making.
